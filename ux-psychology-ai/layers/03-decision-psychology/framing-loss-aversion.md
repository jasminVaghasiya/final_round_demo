# L03-P07 & L03-P08 — Framing and Loss Aversion

---

## Framing Effect (L03-P07)

**Evidence level:** A | **Type:** Empirical effect | **Domain:** Judgment and decision-making

### Definition
People respond differently to logically equivalent information depending on how it is presented — particularly whether it is framed in terms of gains or losses.

### Research Basis
Kahneman and Tversky's (1979, 1981) prospect theory and framing research established that equivalent outcomes framed as gains vs. losses produce different choices. This is one of the most replicated findings in behavioral economics.

### UI Implications
1. **Choose framing deliberately.** "Save 20%" and "Lose 20% by not acting" are equivalent but will produce different response rates.
2. **Deletion, cancellation, and destructive actions should be framed around loss** — to ensure users appreciate the consequence and confirm intentionally.
3. **CTAs that frame action as gaining value convert better than neutral CTAs.** "Get access" vs. "Sign up."
4. **Error messages that frame in terms of what the user can do (gain frame) are more motivating** than messages that dwell on what went wrong (loss frame).
5. **Use framing ethically.** Do not frame choices to manipulate users into decisions against their interests.

## Loss Aversion (L03-P08)

**Evidence level:** A | **Type:** Empirical effect | **Domain:** Decision-making, behavioral economics

### Definition
The psychological impact of losses is felt approximately twice as strongly as equivalent gains. People are more motivated to avoid losses than to achieve equivalent gains.

### Research Basis
Kahneman and Tversky's (1979) prospect theory describes loss aversion as a fundamental characteristic of human risk evaluation. The approximately 2:1 loss-to-gain sensitivity ratio has been reproduced in many contexts, though the specific ratio varies.

### UI Implications
1. **Communicate what users will lose if they do not act.** "Your cart expires in 24 hours" is more motivating than "Come back to buy."
2. **Membership/subscription cancellation flows trigger loss aversion.** Showing what a user will lose on cancellation (features, data, history) is a legitimate retention strategy — but should not misrepresent the reality of cancellation.
3. **Irreversible actions should communicate the loss explicitly.** "This will permanently delete your data and cannot be undone" activates appropriate loss aversion as a protective mechanism.
4. **Error prevention is strengthened by communicating loss.** "Changes will be lost if you navigate away" prompts users to save.
5. **Do not exploit loss aversion to manipulate.** Dark patterns that create artificial urgency or false scarcity exploit loss aversion unethically.
